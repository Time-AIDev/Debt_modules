0. Plain language first

The sheet — the Google Sheet "New Order Master" the team works in; its two working tabs are Done-Collection (leasing) and Done-Cash. PTP — promise to pay: the date and amount a customer commits to. Broken PTP — the date passed without the money. DPD — days past due. Bucket — the sheet's grouping of how many weeks late a customer is. DC Status — the sheet's "debt collection status" dropdown; it records the last action taken, not the money state. Payment Status — the money state: Current, Late, NPL, Closed. NPL — non-performing loan. Next Action — what the officer must do next for a case, and when. LINE OA — the company LINE Official Account, where most customer conversations happen. PEAK — the accounting system where invoices and receipts are created. Odoo — the ERP that holds orders, installation dates and helpdesk tickets. O&M — operations and maintenance, the team that fixes systems. CX / CS — customer service, who open tickets. EPC — the installation crews (VOLT, IPW, SLT, KAT, EMP, RICH and others).

2. The team and its day

Two collection officers and one lead. The lead does the supervisor's and the manager's work at once and sits with the officers, so review today is a conversation across the desk. One officer follows leasing, the other cash; they cover each other when a list is quiet. About 700 customers are installed. The sheet's own pivots show 642 leasing customers (579 Current, 53 Late, 10 NPL) and, on the portfolio summary, 816 contracts with THB 3,189,318.70 overdue; the cash tab shows 42 overdue invoices worth THB 4,438,121.05.

The morning starts in the Done-Collection tab. The first filter is PTP Date equal to today. The second is the Bucket column, looking for customers at two missed instalments who are about to slide toward NPL. After that the officer works down the list by judgement. There is no action-date column, so the question "who called this customer last, and when" cannot be answered from the sheet; the officer opens LINE OA, searches the VOLT ID, and reads the conversation. LINE OA on the standard plan keeps six months, so for a customer installed a year ago the first six months are gone. Some conversations are summarised into the Comment cell, which grows without limit and is read top to bottom.

Contact is by LINE first and phone second. The phone number column exists in the sheet but is hidden because the sheet is shared with other departments. Reminders are sent by hand: the invoice link when the invoice is created, a reminder three days before the due date, a reminder on the due date, and a "please send the slip" message after. The team estimates two thousand or more messages a month and has never counted.

The lead's own words on the sheet: it is the view management wants, not a view for working. Adding a column for the team's own needs is resisted because the sheet is also the report.

3. The sheet as it is used

3.1 Tabs

Working tabs: Done-Collection (leasing, 72 in the tab strip) and Done-Cash (6). Every other tab is an event tab — created for one management question, never removed: Working Sheet, Loan Book, Done Report, Loan Report (five near-identical snapshots with different totals), Repossessed 1, NPL trackers, monthly clones of the order master. The lead wants the working tabs to be the only two that survive.

3.2 How a customer enters the working tab

Orders arrive from Odoo into the order tab ("x"). A formula copies every row whose Order Status is "Order - Done" into Done-Collection or Done-Cash by Order Type. The flip from "Order - EPC Installation" to "Order - Done" is manual and its trigger is not known to the team (it may be the EPC Start Date and EPC End Date columns being filled). Customers who are installed but never flipped do not appear and are not chased. The team member with edit rights flips them by hand when they notice. The sheet shows 907 rows at Order - Done and 182 at Order - EPC Installation.

3.4 The call log that exists inside a cell

A structured string is appended to the Comment cell when a call is logged from the LINE tooling: an opening tag with the VOLT ID, a timestamp and the agent's name, then the contact result, the PTP amount, the PTP date, the next call date and a free note, all separated by vertical bars. Three contact results are used: reached and answered, unreachable and no answer, reached and payment appointment made. This is the only place a "next call" date exists today. Because the separator is a vertical bar inside a spreadsheet cell, every export of the sheet shifts those rows' columns. It is the strongest evidence in the data that the contact log needs a table of its own.

3.5 Formulas whose origin nobody knows

Days Late, Bucket, Payment Status, NPL at 90 days and Late Fees are all formula-driven. The team reads the results and trusts them but cannot say how the late fee is built. The sheet's own "Late Fee Calculation Logic" tab states: 0.3 percent per day on the total unpaid balance, the balance grows by one instalment each month, the due date is the 10th, and the fee is computed in monthly blocks on the cumulative balance. Its worked example reaches THB 41,040 on 190 days late. The "Flow Stages" tab on the same sheet says the due date is the 1st, the late fee starts on day 11 and power cut-off is warned on days 14–15 and executed on day 16. The lead called the daily rate impossible — nine percent a month, 108 percent a year — and said Legal is re-reading the agreement.

4.2 The seventeen steps and where they sit

Steps 1–5 are pre-installation: check the installation plan for the next three days, prepare the invoice data, issue the invoice, deliver it, check on installation day. The workflow narrative marks all five as owned by nobody in the written specification and proposes AR as owner of invoice create, amend and cancel, with Collection owning delivery and follow-up. Steps 6–8 are receipt matching, the monthly invoice run and the pre-due reminders. Steps 9–13 are the daily loop: build the arrears list, contact and record, record and track PTP, handle a service complaint, handle no contact. Steps 14–16 are Manager Review, arrangement or restructure, and NPL, legal and recovery escalation. Step 17 is reporting. The SOP's minimum record standard is that every case answers: status, last contact, what the customer said, PTP date, whether there is an issue, who owns it, what the next action is. "No case without Next Action."

10. What this document does not decide

It records practice and asks. It does not settle the late-fee rate, the grace period, the allocation order, the NPL policy value, the restructure entry gate, who signs the termination letter, who owns the invoice, or the LINE retention plan. Those sit in Case Register v1.4 with owners. It also does not claim the sheet counts are exact: the export is truncated per tab, so every number quoted comes from the sheet's own summary rows.
