0. How to read this record

A blind UAT round is run by testers who have never seen the app, playing the Debt team from the SOP and the seventeen-step workflow only. Every claim is re-run by an independent judge before it counts. Severity: P1 = wrong money on screen or a daily task that cannot be done; P2 = a step the tester could not finish unaided or finished wrongly because the screen misled them; P3 = polish. The stop rule (decision C7) is a full round with no P1, no P2 and at most three P3. Codes such as J2-01 are the judge's finding ids; N2, T2 and S2 are the three testers' own ids. CF1, OD-02 and OQ-44 are open business items in the Case Register. "PEAK" is the accounting system, which the app only ever writes drafts into.

2. What mattered most (the judge's plain words)

  1. The money was still wrong on the screen the officer reads before dialling. Every cash customer was shown owing 80% of an invoice that is fully outstanding, and every late customer was told to ask for less than the instalments missed, because a penalty nobody bills yet was subtracted instead of added. On one customer the app showed a 25,299 instalment for someone who pays 5,799.
  2. One keystroke by the lead took the whole desk down. An unchecked settings value is shared by every session, so the morning list and every case returned an error for minutes and two review requests were lost, while the screen said the save had failed.
  3. The app was quiet at the moments the sheet was loud. A third renegotiation escalated without a word and then refused while still storing the promise; a no-answer call made a broken promise vanish from the morning list; the October link the sheet recorded appeared on a November invoice the app had issued itself.

What worked and is now protected by tests: the seven-tier morning list for both officers and the lead; the drawer refusing to close a call without a next step; the one-contact-per-day lock with its inbound alternative; the ticket returning to the officer's waiting list by itself; the officer to lead and back review round trip; the NPL type landing on the case; the cut-off request asking a reason, refusing when the SOP has not been followed, and restore clearing the status; the two release gates and the refusal to send a link before Accounting approves; the whole release to draft to approved to delivery chain with nothing in the app approving a PEAK document; and the work-book export.

5. The nineteen P3 items

All nineteen were fixed across the two batches and the reconciliation pass. In short: the officer identity is one setting that actually filters the morning list; work-book search returns the row it opens; the ticket box works in either order; a refused act keeps the form and the date the officer typed; PEAK state is read from the answer instead of assumed; a slip can be confirmed from the case itself and the invoice list searches instead of capping at twenty-five; leasing rows carry a confirmable amount hint; month cells and invoices name each other; identities are offered per role; a refusal lists which preconditions were met; the penalty clock and the follow-up status say who set them and why; raw codes and English sentences are gone from the Thai screens; long names no longer collapse the case header; the install window checkbox says what it shows and out-of-window rows are counted separately; closing a ticket records who confirmed the fix; a postponement asks for the new date; the customer menu and its page share one name; a broken promise asks for confirmation and shows its lineage; the reminder set names its offsets; and the morning list sorts by days late within a tier.
